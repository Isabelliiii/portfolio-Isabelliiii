Fire Alarm False Alarm Prediction

Philadelphia, PA · 2024 · with Sihan Yu, Jingqi Lu, Ming Cao

Overview

In Philadelphia’s National Fire Incident Reporting System, 77% of all fire-related incidents are false alarms — system malfunctions, accidental activations, and malicious calls that each still trigger a full emergency response. Every false alarm ties up multiple firefighters, apparatus, and equipment while removing units from availability for real emergencies.

This project builds a logistic regression model to predict the probability that an incoming incident is a false alarm, allowing the fire department to apply graduated response protocols and deploy resources more efficiently.

Data

🔥

Fire Incidents

NFIRS incident records filtered to false alarms (7xx codes) and real fires (1xx, 2xx codes)

📊

Census Demographics

2023 ACS tract-level data: population, income, education, unemployment, racial composition

🏢

Building Certifications

L&I building certification records (Active / Expired / Deficient) joined to census tracts

🌤️

Weather Data

Daily temperature, precipitation, and wind speed matched to incident dates

Methodology

Three logistic regression models were built, each adding a new layer of predictors:

01

Model 1 — Weather Baseline

Temperature, precipitation, wind speed, and building certification rate. Establishes baseline fit. Temperature and precipitation are significant; limited overall explanatory power.

02

Model 2 — + Socioeconomic Context

Adds median income, education rate (BA degree), Black population share, and unemployment rate. AIC drops substantially (67,143 → 59,748), confirming socioeconomic context is a strong signal.

03

Model 3 — Full Model (Best)

Adds temporal indicators (weekend, quarter) and spatial features (proximity to fire station, distance from City Hall). Best overall fit. Selected for threshold analysis and policy use.

Key Findings

Weather

Higher temperatures and any measurable precipitation significantly increase the likelihood of a false alarm — likely due to heat-related system stress and moisture-triggered detectors.

Neighbourhood Context

Higher education levels, larger Black population share, and higher unemployment are all associated with elevated false-alarm odds — indicating structural and socioeconomic patterns in alarm activity.

Building Compliance

Tracts with more active alarm certifications show higher false-alarm rates — suggesting well-monitored buildings generate more nuisance activations than poorly maintained ones.

Spatial & Temporal

False alarms are slightly less likely on weekends, in Q4, and near fire stations. Incidents farther from Center City also show modestly lower false-alarm odds.

Threshold Trade-offs

Logistic regression outputs a probability. A decision threshold converts that probability into a binary classification. Two scenarios:

Threshold = 0.9 · Conservative

96.4%Specificity
8.1%Sensitivity

Identifies only the most extreme false alarms. Minimises risk of misclassifying a real emergency, but misses 42,000+ false alarms.

Threshold = 0.4 · Aggressive

99.95%Sensitivity
0.28%Specificity

Catches almost every false alarm. Enables graduated response — still respond, but with a lighter crew and fewer apparatus.

Conclusions & Recommendations

The full model shows that false alarm activity reflects an interaction of environmental conditions, neighbourhood characteristics, and building system quality — not a single cause. A lower threshold (around 0.4) is recommended operationally: the fire department still responds to every call, but predicted-likely false alarms receive a lighter response package, reducing cost and freeing resources for real emergencies.

Longer-term, the city should strengthen mandatory inspection and compliance enforcement for buildings with repeat violations, and expand community education programmes targeting neighbourhoods and building types with historically high false-alarm rates.
